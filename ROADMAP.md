# COW Farm OS Roadmap

**Blueprint status as of 2026-08-22:** planning is a living system. The commercial concept is sufficiently researched to move into validation, but the launch strategy has been revised from a 40–60 animal start to a **10-cow commercial pilot**. No irreversible farm purchase/build decision should be made until the revised Phase-2 evidence gates below are passed.

## Current strategic design

- Location focus: Badhaiyatal, Bardiya, Lumbini Province, Nepal.
- Launch target: **10 high-quality dairy cows** as a controlled commercial pilot.
- Preferred initial mix: ~8 lactating cows + ~2 advanced-pregnant cows/heifers, subject to live procurement quality.
- Infrastructure: build only the first modular shed/utility stage needed for ~15–20 adult animals, while master-planning roads, drainage, manure flow, utilities and expansion lanes for ~100 total animals.
- Expansion sequence: **10 -> 20 -> 40 -> 60 -> ~100**, with each step performance-gated rather than calendar-gated.
- Underwriting yield: **12–14 L/day per lactating animal average**; do not build the investment case on exceptional genetic potential.
- Genetics: prioritize adapted HF-cross/Jersey-cross cattle. Use sexed semen selectively on fertile, desirable cows/heifers to build internal replacements; retain complete breeding records from Day 1.
- Subsidy policy: **exclude grants from the base-case economics**; use only verified/open FY2083/84 schemes as upside.
- Milk-buyer policy: buyer payment reliability is a first-class underwriting variable, not a secondary operational issue.

## Phase 1 — Farm concept — ✅ REVISED FOR 10-COW PILOT / ENGINEERING INPUTS PENDING

- Starting operating envelope: **10 cows**.
- Design principle: build a low-cost, high-hygiene modular first stage; do not overbuild a 100-cow facility before proving the economics.
- Genetics: HF-cross for volume + Jersey/HF×Jersey resilience cohort; procurement based primarily on what can be independently verified now: current milk yield, age estimate, pregnancy status, udder/teat condition, CMT mastitis status, feet/legs, body condition and veterinary exam. Pedigree/history is a bonus, not a mandatory assumption.
- Feed: own/controlled roughage and silage treated as critical infrastructure. Establish fodder and silage capacity before cattle arrival.
- Farm design: loose housing, quarantine/sick/calving capacity, heat-stress control, reliable water, drainage, manure handling, and expansion lanes.
- Milking: small reliable milking-machine setup is sufficient for 10 cows; large parlour investment deferred.
- Chilling: size only to confirmed buyer collection interval and expected saleable milk volume.
- Future integrations reserved in site plan: vermicompost, biogas, vegetable farming and hydroponics; these are **not Phase-1 complexity** unless they reduce waste/feed cost with a clear payback.

Pending before final engineering freeze:
- Surveyed site.
- Soil/water tests.
- Summer climate/heat-load design values.
- Vendor BOQs and civil quotations.
- Exact modular expansion layout from 10 to 20/40/60/100.

Primary files: `docs/02-herd/`, `docs/03-feed/`, `docs/04-farm-design/`, `docs/09-phase-1/`.

## Phase 2 — Commercial validation — 🔴 NEXT ACTIVE PHASE

No construction or cow purchase until these gates are documented.

### Gate A — Milk market
- Written offers from at least 3 buyers/collection routes where practical.
- Price formula by fat/SNF.
- Rejection/adulteration/antibiotic-residue rules.
- Pickup responsibility and collection timing.
- Payment cycle and security for delayed payment.
- Daily volume acceptance for ~100–140 L/day pilot range and future expansion.
- **Red flag:** national DDC arrears remain material in August 2026; underwrite payment delay explicitly and prefer buyers with demonstrable recent payment behavior.

### Gate B — Feed & land
- Confirm irrigated fodder land owned/leased/contracted for the 10-cow pilot.
- Soil and irrigation test.
- Seasonal fodder calendar.
- Maize silage, Napier/green fodder and dry-roughage yields.
- Delivered concentrate/feed ingredient quotations.
- Nutritionist ration for lactating/dry/heifer/calf groups.
- Build feed inventory before animal arrival; no herd purchase based on future-unsecured fodder assumptions.

### Gate C — Animal procurement
- Build a shortlist of at least 20 candidate cows to select the best 10.
- Live price quotations from multiple sources.
- Independently observed 24-hour-equivalent milk yield where feasible.
- Veterinary pregnancy confirmation for animals sold as pregnant.
- Age estimate by dentition/records.
- CMT mastitis screening, udder/teat examination and feet/leg/gait inspection.
- Disease-screening, quarantine, transport and acclimatization plan.
- Do not reject good cows solely because pedigree/lifetime records are unavailable.

### Gate D — Finance
- Detailed 10-cow CAPEX and 3–6 month working-capital model.
- Bank offers, collateral and repayment schedules.
- Concessional-loan eligibility checked against current NRB procedure; commercial agriculture/livestock remains an eligible concessional-credit category, but actual bank pricing and subsidy availability must be obtained in writing.
- Debt downside scenario at lower milk price/yield, 2–3 month buyer payment delay and higher feed cost.
- Subsidy excluded from debt-service viability.

### Gate E — Insurance & risk
- Insurer valuation and premium quotation for the specific 10 animals before purchase.
- Current government premium subsidy confirmed in writing at policy issuance.
- Require policy issuance through the current Agriculture and Livestock Insurance System (ALIS) where applicable.
- Mortality/disease/heat/power/water/business-interruption risks mapped.

### Gate F — Government programmes
- Badhaiyatal has published FY2083/84 policy/economic documents; Bardiya IALDO has current programme forms/procedures online, but **no new Bardiya dairy-specific FY2083/84 proposal call has yet been verified on the public notice board as of 2026-08-22**.
- Check Badhaiyatal, Bardiya IALDO and Lumbini notices immediately before procurement because new-year calls may open after programme/budget publication.
- No asset purchased early if that could make the expense ineligible for a matching grant.
- No duplicate subsidy claim for the same asset/cost.

### Gate G — Engineering & compliance
- Commercial cow/buffalo welfare standard incorporated in design.
- Local registration/PAN/tax/environmental/municipal requirements confirmed.
- Water, drainage, manure and dead-animal disposal plan approved for the site.
- Reserve clean interfaces for future vermicompost/biogas/hydroponic/vegetable modules without burdening Phase-1 CAPEX.

## Phase 5 — Scale — 🟡 PERFORMANCE-GATED

### 10 -> 20 cows
Proceed only after several consecutive months of evidence showing:
- saleable milk yield at/above underwriting target;
- buyer takes full volume and pays within agreed terms;
- feed cost/litre and gross margin are positive under actual local prices;
- mastitis/mortality within limits;
- breeding/conception performance acceptable;
- fodder/silage system proven through at least one meaningful seasonal cycle or secured by contract;
- working-capital reserve intact;
- labour routines stable.

### 20 -> 40 -> 60 -> ~100
Each scale step requires the same KPI review plus infrastructure/feed/buyer capacity confirmation. Do not assume linear economics.

### ~100 -> 150+
Require a new investment memo, updated buyer contract, feed-capacity audit, management-depth review and processing/chilling study.

Revenue sequencing:
1. Milk.
2. Retained heifers / surplus breeding stock where economical.
3. Compost/vermicompost.
4. Biogas energy/fuel savings.
5. Vegetable farming and/or hydroponics only after dedicated agronomy, market and water/energy economics are proven.
6. Third-party milk collection only if controls and margin justify it.
7. Direct milk/value-added processing only after a separate market/compliance investment case.

Primary file: `docs/07-expansion/SCALE_AND_REVENUE_ROADMAP.md`.
