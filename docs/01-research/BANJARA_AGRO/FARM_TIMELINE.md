# Banjara Agro Farm Timeline

Reviewed: 2026-08-14

This timeline reconciles changing figures across public sources. Figures are reported values, not audited accounts.

## 2021 — build-out / early operating phase

Source: Capital Nepal, 2021.

- Location: Biratnagar-12, Darahiya, Morang.
- Project area: about 5 bigha.
- Farm reported 27 improved cows at the time.
- Breed described as `Holstein Jersey`.
- Target herd: 200 cows.
- Management stated current cows could reach around 30 L/day and intended to breed toward cows capable of ~40 L/day.
- Management explicitly discussed using sexed semen (`सेक्सीबल`, apparently meaning sexed semen) to generate improved heifer calves and eventually supply improved cows to farmers.
- Reported cow milk: about 425 L/day, plus about 100 L/day from 12 buffaloes.
- Milk was being home-delivered; unsold milk converted to curd, ghee and paneer.
- Automation vision included machine feeding/watering, milking and manure handling; biogas was planned.
- Investment projection: project expected to reach ~NPR 50 crore when completed.
- Financing reported: NPR 11 crore debt including NPR 5 crore agricultural credit.

### Interpretation

The 2021 farm was still being built while operating. The `50 crore` number should therefore be treated as a project-completion estimate, not necessarily money already spent at that point.

## 2022 — rapid herd expansion

Sources: RSS/Arthik Awaj, eNepalese, Capital Nepal.

- Reported investment to date: more than NPR 30 crore.
- Land purchase reportedly cost NPR 11 crore for 5 bigha.
- Herd: 112 Indian improved `Holstein Jersey` cows.
- Reported purchase price: ~NPR 250,000/cow.
- Individual yield claim: 20–45 L/day depending on cow.
- Milk sales: ~1,000 L/day.
- About 200 L/day of milk reportedly diverted into curd/paneer production.
- Monthly income claim: ~NPR 15 lakh.
- Direct jobs: ~30.
- Infrastructure: organized sheds, biogas structure, two silage storage centers, feed storage, vegetable integration.
- Technical support: Rajasthan-based technician Surendra Sharma named in reports.
- Grant: approximately NPR 1 crore from Livestock Innovation Project for a milking parlour reported.
- Feed development: first-party social posts show red Napier planting and explicit focus on resolving green-fodder shortage.
- July 2022: first-party post says a Dutch dairy expert visited for 10 days to review practices, identify gaps and recommend improvements.

### Interpretation

The farm scaled herd size much faster than its early phase and simultaneously discovered feed-system constraints. The Napier project and foreign technical review suggest management was actively correcting operational gaps rather than having a perfect system from day one.

## 2023 — mechanized operating model established

Source: Kantipur, July 2023.

- Reported investment: ~NPR 40 crore.
- Herd: 145 Holstein mother cows.
- Milk sales: ~1,100 L/day.
- Individual yield range reported: 17–33 L/day.
- Jobs: 42 total — 32 salaried + 10 daily-wage workers.
- Specialist veterinary support reportedly brought from Punjab, India.
- Automated/mechanized functions: feed/water handling, manure cleaning, milking.
- Feed factory operating on-site.
- 30 bigha contract maize production by 20 farmers in Sunsari/Devanaganj.
- Farm reportedly advanced 40% capital to those maize growers.
- Whole maize plant purchased in-field at NPR 4/kg when suitable for silage.
- Silage stock: ~800 tonnes, reportedly enough until the following Baisakh.
- Annual value of milk + manure sales reported: ~NPR 5 crore.
- Phase 2 plan: add 88 cows, requiring another ~NPR 4 crore.
- Management claimed no infertility in the herd at that time and intended to establish an on-farm health lab.

### Useful calculated indicators (not source claims)

If 145 `mother cows` and ~1,100 L/day sales are interpreted simplistically, milk sold per mother cow averages only ~7.6 L/day. That does **not** mean each lactating cow gave 7.6 L; dry cows, pregnant cows, non-lactating animals, calves, retained milk and processing can materially change the denominator. This is why future benchmarking must distinguish total herd, adult females, lactating cows, saleable milk and milk used internally.

## 2024 — processing/testing equipment deepening

Public trade-record samples show imports including:

- 500 LPH cream separator.
- Milk analyzer MA-815ABS.

### Interpretation

The business appears to be moving beyond raw milk into basic dairy separation/quality-testing capability. Public trade data is incomplete, so this is directional evidence rather than a complete equipment list.

## 2025 — internal breeding and herd self-replacement visible

Sources: Kantipur, World Bank, trade data.

- Herd: around 180 cattle including calves.
- Management says farm began with 8 Jersey cows around five years earlier.
- Farm reports it now breeds its own cattle and also sells cows, reducing dependence on purchasing cattle from India.
- Milk production: ~1,300 L/day.
- Monthly income: ~NPR 15 lakh reported.
- Direct employment: 50+.
- Infrastructure noted: milking parlour, silage/feed storage, biogas/manure handling, 3,000 L chiller, cow-shower system.
- World Bank identifies Banjara Agro as a successful venture supported by NLSIP.
- March 2025 trade record: maize silage baller imported from India.

### Interpretation

The most strategically important change is not the extra milk; it is **moving from buying animals to producing replacements internally**. This reduces biosecurity/import risk and allows selection of genetics suited to the farm's management and climate.

# What the timeline tells us

1. Banjara was not born fully automated. It evolved from a small starting herd while infrastructure, feed and genetics were built around it.
2. Feed became a strategic business system: own/contract maize, silage storage, Napier, feed factory, later silage-baling equipment.
3. Genetics moved from purchasing improved cows toward sexed-semen/breeding ambition and then internal cow production.
4. Capital intensity is very high relative to milk volume because Banjara includes land, infrastructure, automation, dairy processing, feed systems and broader integrated-agriculture ambitions.
5. Reported headline yields (e.g. 20–45 L/cow) should never be used as the average herd yield without lactating-cow counts and test-day records.
6. Labour did not disappear with automation: reported jobs rose from ~30 to 42 to 50+ as the farm expanded.
7. The farm diversified revenue and infrastructure into manure, milk products, feed/fodder systems, cattle sales and integrated agriculture.
