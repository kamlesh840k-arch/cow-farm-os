# Banjara Agro — Hydroponics / Hydroponic Fodder Check

**Reviewed:** 2026-08-19

## Conclusion

**No credible public evidence was found that Banjara Agro currently operates a hydroponic fodder system or hydroponic crop-production unit.**

What is publicly documented instead:

1. Banjara Agro describes its dairy-feed strategy around **corn silage and concentrate feed** through Kunti Cattle Fodder Company.
2. Banjara Agro also describes plans/work with overseas partners for a **Greenhouse Park** for modern vegetable and fruit production.
3. A 2025 Nepali media report says vegetables are grown with **temperature control** on its land.
4. Greenhouse cultivation / controlled-environment agriculture is **not automatically hydroponics**. No source located in this review explicitly says that Banjara's greenhouse or vegetable production is soil-less, NFT, DWC, aeroponic, or hydroponic.
5. Targeted searches of indexed YouTube/web results for `Banjara Agro hydroponic`, `hydroponic fodder`, and Nepali equivalents did not produce a Banjara hydroponic-fodder source.

## Confidence

**MEDIUM-HIGH** for the statement: "There is no publicly documented hydroponic fodder system found in the indexed sources reviewed."

This is not proof that Banjara has never trialed an unpublicized, unindexed, private, deleted, or small-scale hydroponic system.

## Sources checked

- Banjara Agro official website — Banjara Agro / portfolio pages
- Kunti Cattle Fodder official page
- Banjara Agro Group official/LinkedIn material
- Nepali media reporting on Banjara's fodder and vegetable activities
- Targeted English and Nepali web/YouTube searches for hydroponics and hydroponic fodder

## Relevance to COW Farm OS

Banjara should **not** be used as evidence that hydroponic fodder is economically proven for its dairy operation.

For our own farm, hydroponic fodder should be evaluated independently against:
- maize silage,
- Napier/perennial green fodder,
- seasonal wheat/oat/maize fodder,
- concentrate displacement,
- dry-matter yield,
- seed cost,
- labour,
- water/electricity,
- mold/hygiene risk,
- milk-response economics.

Nepal NARC research has evaluated hydroponic maize, oat and wheat fodder, so the technology can be assessed using Nepal-specific data rather than copying Banjara.
