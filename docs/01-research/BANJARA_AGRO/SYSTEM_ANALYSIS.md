# Banjara Agro System Analysis

Reviewed: 2026-08-14

## Executive conclusion

Banjara Agro is best understood not as a `large cow shed`, but as an integrated dairy-production system built around five linked capabilities:

1. genetics and replacement breeding,
2. feed security,
3. mechanized daily operations,
4. milk handling/processing,
5. manure + integrated agriculture.

The main lesson for our Bardiya project is to copy the **system architecture**, not Banjara's capital scale.

## 1. Genetics and breeding

### Evidence

- 2021 reporting describes the cattle as `Holstein Jersey` and quotes management planning sexed-semen use to produce high-quality female calves.
- 2022 sources still use `Holstein Jersey` terminology and report Indian-origin improved cows.
- 2023 Kantipur uses `Holstein` for 145 mother cows.
- 2025 Kantipur describes approximately 180 Holstein-Jersey cattle including calves and says the farm now produces its own cattle and sells some.

### Important caution

Public reporting uses breed names imprecisely. `Holstein`, `Holstein Jersey`, and `Jersey` appear across different years. This is insufficient to determine purebred status or exact HF/Jersey blood percentage.

### Lesson for our farm

Do not buy animals based on appearance or a seller saying `HF`. Require animal-level records where possible: age, parity, last calving, pregnancy status, test-day milk, mastitis history, body condition, sire/semen identity, vaccination and reproduction records.

## 2. Reproduction strategy

### Evidence

- Early management explicitly discussed sexed-semen-based heifer production.
- By 2025 the farm reports producing its own replacement cattle rather than depending on Indian purchases.
- 2023 management claimed no infertility at the farm at that moment; this is a management claim and not independently audited.

### Lesson

The key breeding KPI is not twins or `babies per cow`. It is:

- pregnancy rate,
- calving interval,
- age at first calving,
- live female calves per 100 eligible females,
- heifer survival to first calving,
- replacement rate,
- genetic merit of retained daughters.

Our future herd-growth model should be built from these KPIs.

## 3. Feed security is central

### Evidence

Banjara has progressively invested in:

- corn silage,
- concentrate feed,
- its own/contract maize supply,
- red Napier,
- feed-processing capacity,
- silage storage,
- silage film manufacturing through Darahiya Plastics,
- maize silage baler import in 2025.

In 2023, Kantipur reported about 800 tonnes of silage in stock and 30 bigha of maize contracted from 20 farmers, with Banjara advancing 40% of capital and purchasing whole maize plants for silage.

### Lesson

Feed is not an input to solve after buying cows. Herd size must be constrained by secured annual dry-matter supply. Before purchasing our first large batch of animals, we should calculate:

- annual green fodder requirement,
- silage tonnes,
- dry fodder,
- concentrate requirement,
- storage capacity,
- fodder acreage,
- drought/flood reserve,
- cost per kg dry matter.

Because our project has access to land, feed self-reliance may become one of our strongest cost advantages.

## 4. Mechanization

### Evidence

Reports repeatedly describe mechanized/automated:

- milking,
- feed/water handling,
- manure cleaning,
- cow showering,
- milk chilling/handling.

A 2025 report names a 3,000 L chiller and milking parlour. Trade data also indicates milk-testing/separation and silage-baling equipment.

### Lesson

Mechanization should be purchased only when it reduces one of these:

- labour cost,
- inconsistency,
- hygiene risk,
- feed wastage,
- milk loss,
- animal stress,
- expansion bottlenecks.

Do not copy expensive automation simply because Banjara has it. Design our lanes, power, drainage, feed alleys and milking flow so machinery can be added later without rebuilding the farm.

## 5. Labour

### Evidence

Despite mechanization, reported employment rose:

- ~30 workers in 2022,
- 42 in 2023,
- 50+ in 2025.

### Lesson

Automation does not necessarily mean a tiny workforce in an integrated farm. Banjara also operates feed, dairy products, vegetables, maintenance, breeding and other functions. We need labour productivity measured per function rather than copying worker counts.

## 6. Milk economics

### Reported snapshots

- 2021: roughly 425 L/day cow milk plus ~100 L/day buffalo milk in early phase.
- Early expansion report: ~800 L/day with 1,500 L/day near-term target.
- 2022: ~1,000 L/day milk sales.
- 2023: ~1,100 L/day milk sales.
- 2025: ~1,300 L/day production.

### Critical caution

Public reports often quote impressive individual-cow yields (20–45 L/day) but do not give the number of cows actually in milk. We cannot calculate true average lactating-cow yield without:

- lactating-cow count,
- dry-cow count,
- fresh-cow count,
- milk fed to calves,
- milk processed internally,
- discarded/withheld milk,
- exact measurement period.

### Lesson

Our dashboard should track both:

`litres per lactating cow per day`

and

`litres sold per total adult female per day`.

The second exposes reproductive/dry-period inefficiency that headline cow-yield figures hide.

## 7. Manure and circular integration

### Evidence

Banjara reports:

- manure handling,
- biogas structure,
- organic fertilizer use,
- vegetable integration,
- manure sales in the 2023 revenue description.

### Lesson

For our farm, dung should be modeled as a resource stream from day one:

- biogas,
- slurry,
- compost/vermicompost,
- fertilizer for fodder crops,
- possible external sales.

But manure revenue should remain a conservative side-income assumption until local demand and processing cost are verified.

## 8. Processing and market strategy

### Evidence

Banjara has reported:

- home delivery,
- curd,
- paneer,
- ghee,
- chilling,
- cream separation,
- milk analysis,
- broader farm-to-home retail.

### Lesson

Raw milk offers simpler operations but limited margin control. Processing can increase value but introduces food safety, packaging, refrigeration, branding and distribution complexity. We should phase processing only after milk production is stable.

## 9. Capital structure

Public numbers vary by year and context:

- ~NPR 8 crore early investment mentioned in one early report,
- debt/credit figures including ~NPR 11 crore reported in 2021,
- >NPR 30 crore invested by 2022,
- ~NPR 40 crore invested by 2023,
- early projected completion cost around NPR 50 crore.

This is not necessarily contradictory: Banjara was built in phases, and some reports discuss actual spend while others discuss planned total project cost.

### Lesson

For us, capital must be separated into:

1. land (already available vs purchased),
2. livestock,
3. sheds/civil works,
4. milking/chilling,
5. feed production/storage,
6. power/water/cooling,
7. manure/biogas,
8. vehicles/machinery,
9. working capital,
10. contingency.

Banjara's land purchase alone was reported around NPR 11 crore, which makes direct `total investment per cow` comparison misleading for our project if we already control land.

## 10. What appears worth copying

- Feed-first planning.
- Silage as strategic inventory, not emergency feed.
- Internal replacement breeding.
- Sexed semen on selected females if economics/conception performance work locally.
- Cooling and cow comfort for Terai conditions.
- Mechanized milking and hygienic chilling at appropriate scale.
- Farm layout built for future automation.
- Manure integration.
- Farmer contract model for maize if own land cannot supply enough.
- Outside specialist review to identify blind spots.
- Animal records and future on-farm diagnostics.

## 11. What we should NOT automatically copy

- Very high capital spend before unit economics are proven.
- Large herd expansion before feed security.
- Assuming every high-yield animal is profitable in Terai heat.
- Using claimed peak yields as average production assumptions.
- Building processing capacity too early.
- Buying machinery without throughput justification.
- Depending on grants as the foundation of project viability.

## 12. Highest-priority unknowns still to verify directly

1. Exact breed/blood percentage and semen suppliers.
2. Current lactating vs dry vs heifer vs calf composition.
3. Average 305-day lactation yield.
4. Days open, services/conception, conception rate and calving interval.
5. Age at first calving.
6. Calf mortality and heifer survival.
7. Exact ration and dry-matter intake by group.
8. Feed cost per litre.
9. Milk fat/SNF and rejection/mastitis rates.
10. Shed dimensions, cow space, bedding and cooling performance.
11. Milking parlour make/model/capacity.
12. Labour allocation by function.
13. Actual annual operating profit/cash flow rather than revenue.
14. Debt service burden.
15. Current status of buffalo operations, if any.

## Bottom line for COW Farm OS

Banjara Agro should be our **benchmark case**, not our blueprint. The transferable advantage is its integrated thinking: genetics + feed + cow comfort + mechanization + milk handling + manure + scale. Our goal should be to achieve comparable operational discipline with much lower capital per productive litre in Bardiya.
