# Phase 0 — Research & Feasibility

**Status:** Planning complete; external commercial validation still required before investment approval.

## Feasibility conclusion

**Conditional GO** for a 30-breeding-female commercial dairy in Bardiya, provided the Phase-2 gates are met. The project should not depend on subsidy, unusually high milk prices, or pure-Holstein peak-yield assumptions.

## Verified anchors

- Nepal's National Dairy Development Board publishes a minimum raw-milk price of NPR 65/L for milk at 4 fat and 8 SNF.
- Bardiya-specific research comparing 262 cows found peak milk yield around 14.9 L/day for Holstein cross and 13.5 L/day for Jersey cross, while reported inter-calving interval was longer for Holstein cross (14.9 months) than Jersey cross (13.5 months).
- Nepal research confirms increasing temperature-humidity index reduces milk production, with Holstein crosses more heat-sensitive than Jersey cattle/crosses.
- Banjara Agro demonstrates the value of integrating genetics, silage, cooling, mechanized milking, chilling and replacement-cattle production; its capital intensity should not be copied at Phase 1.
- The Integrated Agriculture & Livestock Development Office, Bardiya currently provides veterinary/laboratory services and maintains grant/program procedures and proposal forms.
- As of this review, no current FY 2083/84 Bardiya dairy-grant proposal call was identified on the office notice board. Subsidy is therefore treated as upside only.

## Commercial feasibility model

Base design: 30 adult breeding females, site sized for 50 adults.

- Average lactating fraction: 77%
- Saleable milk at steady state: ~415 L/day
- Annual milk: ~151,300 L
- Cash OPEX: ~NPR 83.8 lakh/year
- Cash production cost: ~NPR 55.4/L before depreciation, owner salary, interest and tax
- Initial funding envelope excluding land: ~NPR 174.4 lakh including working capital

Milk-only annual cash operating surplus:
- NPR 65/L: ~NPR 14.6 lakh
- NPR 72.5/L: ~NPR 25.9 lakh
- NPR 77.5/L: ~NPR 33.5 lakh

These are operating surpluses, not accounting profit.

## Phase-2 GO gates

All must be satisfied before animal procurement:

1. Written milk off-take route for at least 450 L/day at launch capacity, with fat/SNF formula, rejection rules and payment cycle documented.
2. Expected blended realized milk price >= NPR 70/L **or** validated cash production cost <= NPR 52/L.
3. 8–10 bigha reliably irrigated fodder capacity available before herd arrival.
4. At least 90 days of roughage/silage reserve before the first purchased cow enters the farm.
5. Foundation animals independently examined and individually documented.
6. CAPEX quotations do not exceed the approved envelope by >10% without a new investment decision.
7. Debt limited to <=40% of initial funding unless milk price/cost performance materially exceeds the base case.
8. Cooling, water, drainage, quarantine and manure systems complete before herd arrival.

## No-go triggers

- Buyer cannot absorb 400–450 L/day.
- Farm relies on a grant to reach break-even.
- No reliable irrigation for fodder.
- Cow seller refuses pregnancy, lactation, disease or identification records.
- Debt >50% of project cost under the current operating-margin assumptions.
- Shed design omits heat mitigation or separate quarantine/calving areas.
