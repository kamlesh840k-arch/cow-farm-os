# Breed Strategy

## Status

**Open research question — no breed has been selected yet.**

## Objective

Choose cattle genetics that maximize lifetime farm profit under Bardiya/Terai conditions, not simply peak daily milk yield.

## Selection criteria

- Milk yield and lactation persistence
- Fertility and calving interval
- Heat tolerance
- Feed efficiency
- Disease and mastitis resistance
- Hoof/leg durability
- Calving ease
- Productive lifespan
- Availability and quality of semen/genetics in Nepal
- Replacement-heifer economics
- Milk fat/protein where buyer pricing makes them relevant

## Breeds/genetic options to investigate

- Holstein Friesian (HF)
- HF crosses at different blood percentages
- Jersey
- Jersey crosses
- Suitable indigenous/local genetics
- Other dairy genetics proven under tropical/subtropical conditions

## Reproduction principles under study

- Target one healthy calf per successful pregnancy; twinning is not a production objective.
- Retain daughters selectively from the best-performing females.
- Evaluate sex-sorted semen for genetically valuable heifers/cows.
- Track age at first calving, services per conception, pregnancy rate and calving interval.
- Do not breed replacements from animals with persistent health, fertility or structural problems unless there is a compelling genetic reason.

## Required evidence before decision

1. Climate suitability for Bardiya.
2. Locally achievable milk production, not foreign benchmark yield alone.
3. Feed requirement and cost per litre.
4. Fertility under heat stress.
5. Local semen/AI availability and quality.
6. Purchase price and health status of foundation animals.
7. Expected culling/replacement rate.
8. Five-year herd-growth and cash-flow impact.
