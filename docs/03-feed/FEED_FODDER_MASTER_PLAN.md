# Feed & Fodder Master Plan

## Objective

Make feed security the first production system, not an afterthought. The farm should be able to feed animals through heat, monsoon disruption and market-price spikes without emergency ration changes.

## Phase-1 land target

For 30 adult breeding females plus youngstock:
- **4.5–5 bigha silage maize** under reliable irrigation and crop rotation.
- **3.5–4 bigha perennial green fodder** (e.g. suitable Napier/hybrid grass after local agronomic validation).
- **1–2 bigha flexible seasonal fodder/legume/oats/nursery/recovery area**.

Total planning target: **8–10 bigha productive irrigated fodder area**.

For 50 adult females plus youngstock: plan **15–18 bigha** unless contracted roughage/silage is intentionally used.

## Steady-state feed requirement at 30 adults

Planning daily requirement including youngstock:
- silage: ~607 kg/day,
- green fodder: ~543 kg/day,
- straw/dry roughage: ~171 kg/day,
- concentrate: ~184 kg/day.

Annual approximate requirements:
- silage fed: ~221 t,
- green fodder fed: ~198 t,
- straw: ~62 t,
- concentrate: ~67 t.

Allow at least 15% loss between field harvest, storage and feed-out when sizing silage crop requirement.

## Illustrative ration framework

This is a planning ration only; a nutritionist must formulate against actual feed analysis.

| Group | Silage kg | Green kg | Straw kg | Concentrate kg |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Lactating | 18 | 12 | 4 | 6 |
| Dry cow | 12 | 15 | 5 | 2 |
| Heifer | 8 | 12 | 3 | 1.5 |
| Calf | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1.2 |

Mineral mixture, salt and buffers are added by ration formulation and milk yield.

## Silage system

1. Plant in staggered blocks to reduce harvest risk.
2. Harvest at correct whole-plant stage for fermentation.
3. Chop uniformly.
4. Pack rapidly and densely.
5. Exclude air immediately.
6. Design bunker width so daily face removal is adequate; do not build a huge bunker that leaves the face exposed for weeks.
7. Record each pit/bunker: crop, field, harvest date, estimated tonnes, opening date and spoilage.
8. Maintain 90-day feed inventory minimum before scaling herd.

## Feed testing

Quarterly or batch-based testing should include where available:
- dry matter,
- crude protein,
- fiber fractions,
- energy estimate,
- silage pH/fermentation quality,
- mycotoxin risk when suspected.

Bardiya's Integrated Agriculture & Livestock Development Office publishes feed-testing service availability in its citizen charter.

## Procurement policy

Concentrate and mineral feed must be purchased against:
- batch ID,
- manufacturer,
- guaranteed analysis,
- manufacturing/expiry date,
- invoice,
- visual/smell check,
- sample retention for major batches where practical.

## Feed-cost controls

Targets:
- feed <=70% of cash operating cost,
- ration change only after cost-per-litre analysis,
- track feed cost per lactating cow and per litre weekly,
- no concentrate increase unless incremental milk margin justifies it,
- no emergency green-fodder purchase as a normal operating practice.

## Banjara lesson applied

Banjara's strongest transferable lesson is feed integration: secure maize/silage volume before herd expansion. Our version stays smaller, keeps fixed equipment light, and measures local crop yields before buying large TMR/mixing machinery.
