# Hydroponic Fodder Economics — 50 kg/day Pilot for 10 Cows (Nepal)

**Status:** Planning model / quote validation required  
**Date:** 2026-08-19  
**Farm phase:** 10-cow startup  
**Decision:** Pilot-scale only; hydroponics should supplement, not replace, the basal roughage ration.

## 1. Evidence anchors

Nepal NARC/Khumaltar research compared maize, oat and wheat in a locally constructed hydroponic polyhouse. Fresh-fodder yield per kg grain was reported as 5.32 kg for maize, 7.96 kg for oat and 6.76 kg for wheat. The reported historical production costs were NPR 20.64/kg for maize, NPR 24.67/kg for oat and NPR 18.76/kg for wheat. Source: Upreti, Ghimire & Banskota (2022), Journal of Agriculture and Natural Resources, DOI 10.3126/janr.v5i1.50378.

A low-cost hydroponic maize study reported about 1.53 L irrigation water per kg fresh fodder and a seed:fodder ratio of 1:4.57 over an 8-day cycle. For design, this model uses the Nepal NARC maize conversion of 1:5.32 and 1.5 L water/kg fresh fodder.

Recent Nepal market evidence (Bhimad silage operation, 2025) reported approximately NPR 4/kg raw Napier, NPR 12/kg Napier silage and NPR 16/kg maize silage. These are benchmarks, not guaranteed delivered prices at our farm.

Nepal's statutory minimum remuneration effective 17 July 2025 is NPR 754/day and NPR 101/hour (NPR 107/hour for part-time workers). This model uses NPR 101/hour for incremental hydroponic labour.

## 4. Daily operating model

### Base assumptions

- Fresh output: 50 kg/day.
- Seed requirement: 9.398 kg/day based on NARC 5.32× maize fresh-yield ratio.
- Seed price is uncertain; use sensitivity cases of NPR 25 / 40 / 55 per kg until local feed-grade, high-germination maize is quoted.
- CAPEX depreciation: NPR 120,000 over 5 years = NPR 65.75/day.
- Incremental labour: 1 hour/day × NPR 101/hour = NPR 101/day.
- Electricity: NPR 10/day planning allowance for intermittent pump/timer operation; actual farm meter tariff and kWh must replace this after commissioning.
- Water + sanitation consumables: NPR 12/day planning allowance.

## 6. Break-even logic

Because 9.398 kg seed is needed for 50 kg output, each NPR 1/kg change in maize seed price changes the hydroponic fodder production cost by approximately:

`9.398 / 50 = NPR 0.188 per kg fresh fodder`

Therefore seed procurement is the largest controllable variable.

With the non-seed cost assumptions in this model, hydroponic fresh fodder costs about NPR 3.78/kg before seed. Approximate seed-price break-even against alternatives is:

- Against NPR 12/kg Napier silage: seed can cost roughly **NPR 43.8/kg** before hydroponic exceeds NPR 12/kg fresh.
- Against NPR 16/kg maize silage: seed can cost roughly **NPR 65/kg** before hydroponic exceeds NPR 16/kg fresh.
- Hydroponics cannot compete with NPR 4/kg raw Napier in this model unless most labour/capex cost is treated as sunk and seed is extremely cheap.

These are fresh-weight break-evens only. On dry matter, conventional fodder/silage remains economically stronger in most normal circumstances.

## 7. Why hydroponics can still make sense

Hydroponics should be judged as a **fodder-security tool**, not just by NPR/kg fresh matter. It can provide value through:

1. Reliable green feed during winter/drought/market shortage.
2. Very small land footprint.
3. Predictable daily harvest.
4. Low irrigation-water requirement compared with field green fodder.
5. Ability to maintain some succulent feed when land is being developed or forage crops fail.
6. Potential palatability benefit and ration variety.

Those benefits do not justify replacing long-fibre roughage, silage/hay or a properly balanced dairy ration.

## 8. Proposed 10-cow feeding strategy

Start at **3 kg fresh hydroponic fodder/cow/day** (30 kg/day total) after gradual adaptation. If intake, dung consistency, milk response and hygiene remain normal, test **5 kg/cow/day** (50 kg/day total).

Do not count 5 kg hydroponic fresh fodder as equivalent to 5 kg Napier, silage or other roughage. Balance the full ration on dry matter, energy, protein, effective fibre, minerals and the cow's milk yield/body weight.

## 9. 30-day pilot measurement protocol

Record every day:

- seed kg loaded;
- seed purchase price and supplier;
- germination percentage;
- water litres;
- pump kWh (or run-time if a separate meter is unavailable);
- labour minutes;
- tray harvest kg;
- rejected/moldy kg;
- usable hydroponic fodder kg;
- fodder offered and refused by cow/group;
- milk litres/cow;
- concentrate kg/cow;
- other green fodder/silage kg;
- health/dung observations.

Calculate weekly:

- actual kg fresh fodder / kg seed;
- rejection percentage;
- NPR/kg usable fresh fodder;
- estimated NPR/kg DM;
- labour minutes/kg;
- water L/kg;
- milk change versus pre-pilot baseline;
- cost per litre of milk before vs during trial.

## 10. Go / no-go thresholds

### GO / scale toward 50 kg/day if

- Fresh conversion is consistently >= 5.0 kg usable fodder/kg seed.
- Mold/rejection stays below 5%.
- Base operating cost remains <= NPR 12/kg fresh at actual seed price.
- Labour stays <= 60 minutes/day.
- Cows consume it readily without reducing required long-fibre intake.
- Milk margin or fodder-security value justifies the incremental cost.

### HOLD / redesign if

- Conversion is 4.0–5.0×.
- Mold/rejection is 5–10%.
- Labour exceeds 60–90 minutes/day.
- Cost is NPR 12–16/kg fresh without measurable ration benefit.

### NO-GO / do not scale if

- Conversion remains below 4×.
- Repeated mold/fermentation occurs.
- Usable cost exceeds NPR 16/kg fresh under normal fodder availability.
- The system causes conventional forage production to be neglected.

## 11. Procurement rules

Before purchasing the 50 kg/day system:

1. Obtain at least three local quotes for feed-grade maize with germination test results.
2. Do not use expensive hybrid planting seed merely for fodder production unless its economics are demonstrably superior.
3. Buy a small batch and run a tray germination test before bulk purchase.
4. Test for visible fungal contamination and reject musty grain.
5. Prefer washable reusable trays and corrosion-resistant rack material.
6. Design drainage so standing water cannot accumulate.
7. Ensure cross-ventilation; do not solve mold by simply increasing misting.
8. Design capacity with ~10–15% spare trays for cleaning and failures.

## 12. Current decision for Cow Farm OS

**Recommended:** build a low-cost/semi-automatic pilot capable of 30–50 kg/day, but initially operate at 30 kg/day. Budget ceiling for the first installation: **approximately NPR 120,000–150,000 before supplier quotes**.

The farm should simultaneously plan conventional forage (especially Napier/seasonal green fodder) and silage storage. Hydroponic fodder is a supplementary resilience layer, not the foundation of the feed system.
