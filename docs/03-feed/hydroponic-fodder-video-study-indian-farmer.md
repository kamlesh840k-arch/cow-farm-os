# Hydroponic Fodder Study — Indian Farmer Video

**Status:** Research note / decision input  
**Farm context:** Phase-1 start with 10 cows in Nepal  
**Primary video:** Indian Farmer — “कम जगह, कम पानी में झटपट मिलेगा चारा | Hydroponic Fodder System का कमाल”  
**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=g2SG_2_tJG8  
**Video date:** 31 Oct 2025 (indexed web metadata)  
**Studied:** 2026-08-19

## 1. Executive conclusion

Hydroponic fodder is technically relevant to Cow Farm OS, especially as a **supplementary, year-round green-feed system** where land, water, winter fodder, or reliable fresh-fodder supply is constrained.

It should **not** be evaluated using the marketing-style statement “1 kg seed becomes 6–8 kg fodder” alone. Most of the increase is water. The economic unit that matters is:

> **NPR per kg of usable dry matter / nutrients delivered to the cow, plus any measurable milk or health benefit.**

For a 10-cow Phase-1 farm, preferred strategy is a **low-cost or semi-automatic modular pilot** rather than a high-capex fully automated unit.

## 2. What the video demonstrates

The video is specifically about **hydroponic green fodder for livestock**, rather than nutrient-solution hydroponics for vegetables.

Indexed chapter metadata includes:

- 00:00 — Introduction
- 03:33 — Animals for which hydroponic fodder can be used
- 04:02 — Hydroponic fodder setup
- remaining sections cover production/operation and practical system discussion

The system concept:

1. Select clean cereal grain seed.
2. Soak/germinate seed.
3. Spread germinated seed into shallow trays.
4. Arrange trays vertically on racks.
5. Irrigate/mist repeatedly while ensuring drainage and ventilation.
6. Grow the root-shoot mat without soil.
7. Harvest the complete mat after roughly one week (exact cycle varies with crop/system).
8. Feed fresh as part of the ration.

## 3. Claims that are well supported

### Very short production cycle

Scientific literature reports hydroponic green fodder production in roughly **5–10 days**. Low-cost systems commonly report **5–8 kg fresh fodder/kg seed in 7–8 days** under suitable management.

### Small land footprint

Vertical racks allow several growing days to occupy the same floor footprint, making the system useful where dedicated fodder land is limited.

### Lower water requirement than field-grown green fodder

Recent systematic literature reports large water savings in well-managed hydroponic systems, especially where irrigation water is efficiently applied/recovered.

### Year-round output is possible

Because the growing environment can be sheltered and partially controlled, production can continue when field fodder is seasonally constrained.

### Suitable crops

Common cereals include:

- maize
- barley
- wheat
- oats

Crop choice should be decided by **local seed price, germination %, contamination risk, fresh yield, dry-matter yield and animal response**, not fresh biomass alone.

## 4. The most important economic warning

A large gain in **fresh weight is not the same as a large gain in nutrients**.

Hydroponic sprouts absorb a large amount of water. Dry matter percentage is therefore low, and dry-matter losses can occur during germination due to respiration.

A Nepal NARC dairy study reported hydroponic maize fodder at approximately:

- Dry matter: **12.39%**
- Crude protein: **12.55% of DM**
- NDF: **47.04% of DM**
- ADL: **16.51% of DM**

Example implication:

- 7 kg fresh hydroponic fodder at 12.39% DM contains only about **0.87 kg dry matter**.

Therefore we must never compare:

> 1 kg dry maize seed → 7 kg green mat

and conclude that feed quantity increased sevenfold.

The correct comparison is:

> seed cost + water + electricity + labour + depreciation + spoilage **per kg DM and per unit of energy/protein actually fed**.

## 5. Nepal-specific evidence

### NARC/Khumaltar lactating-cattle experiment

Upreti, Tiwari, Ghimire and Banskota studied six lactating Jersey/Holstein-cross cattle in Nepal.

Key points:

- average body weight around 387 kg
- initial milk yield around 8 L/day
- conventional ration compared with a ration supplemented with hydroponic maize fodder
- hydroponic treatment described as 88% conventional green fodder/concentrate ration + 12% hydroponic maize fodder
- hydroponic maize grown in a 12 ft × 8 ft × 9 ft polyhouse with fogging/sprinkler irrigation
- clean maize soaked overnight, held in jute sack for germination, then transferred to trays
- harvested around day 11 at 20–30 cm in this particular trial
- milk yield was numerically higher in the hydroponic group in parts of the trial, but the difference was **not statistically significant (P > 0.05)**
- authors described a positive health response, but the experiment was small and short; it is not strong enough evidence to promise a fixed milk increase

The reported HMF production cost was NPR 20.62/kg in that historical experiment. **Do not use this as a 2026 budget figure** because seed, labour and electricity assumptions are old.

## 6. What recent systematic evidence says

A 2026 systematic review covering 92 studies concluded that:

- hydroponic fodder is technically feasible
- manual, semi-automatic and fully automated systems all exist
- low-cost systems can use local racks/trays and simple irrigation
- hydroponic fodder works best as a **supplement / partial replacement**, not automatically as a complete ration replacement
- excessive inclusion can reduce dry-matter intake because the feed is bulky and water-rich
- economic feasibility is highly context-dependent
- low-cost and semi-automatic systems are particularly relevant for smallholder/peri-urban livestock farms
- hygiene, ventilation, water quality and mold control are critical

## 7. Main risks

### Mold and fungal contamination — HIGH PRIORITY

Dense wet grain mats plus poor ventilation can quickly create fungal contamination.

Controls required:

- high-germination clean seed
- strict tray washing/disinfection
- clean water
- no standing water
- correct seed density
- good ventilation
- remove contaminated trays immediately
- clean irrigation tank/pipes/nozzles

Moldy fodder should **not be fed** to cows.

### Hidden seed economics

Grain is a valuable feed input. Sprouting it is not automatically cheaper than feeding the grain or growing conventional fodder.

### Labour

Manual systems require daily:

- seed cleaning/soaking
- loading trays
- moving trays by age
- irrigation supervision
- harvesting
- tray washing/sanitizing

### Climate control

High heat/humidity can increase contamination. In colder periods germination/growth may slow. Nepal system design must be adapted to the actual farm elevation and seasonal climate.

### Over-replacement of the ration

Hydroponic fodder is not a substitute for proper ration formulation. Cows still require adequate:

- dry matter
- effective fibre
- energy
- protein
- minerals
- concentrate according to milk production
- clean drinking water

## 8. Recommended Phase-1 design for 10 cows

### Decision: PILOT / ADOPT CONDITIONALLY

Do **not** initially buy a large fully automatic commercial system.

Build or procure a modular **semi-automatic rack + tray + timer irrigation/fogging system**.

### Initial production target

Design the pilot around approximately **40–60 kg fresh hydroponic fodder/day**.

This allows an indicative test range of roughly **4–6 kg fresh fodder/cow/day for 10 cows** while keeping hydroponic fodder supplementary rather than making it the entire green-fodder ration.

This is a **pilot operating target, not a final veterinary ration prescription**. Final inclusion should be set against body weight, milk yield, total ration DM and locally available forage/concentrates.

### Seed requirement planning range

At 5–8 kg fresh fodder per kg seed:

- 40 kg/day output → about **5–8 kg seed/day**
- 60 kg/day output → about **7.5–12 kg seed/day**

For budget modelling, use a conservative conversion initially (e.g. 5–6 kg fresh fodder/kg seed) until our own trays demonstrate actual yield.

### Rack cycle

For a 7–8 day system, the rack must contain one production batch for every growing day. Daily harvest quantity determines daily seed loading and tray count.

Example design logic:

`Daily target fodder ÷ measured kg fodder/tray = trays harvested/day`

`trays harvested/day × crop cycle days = active growing trays`

Add spare trays for washing, sanitation and failed batches.

## 9. What we should measure during the farm pilot

Every batch/day should record:

| KPI | Unit |
|---|---:|
| Seed loaded | kg/day |
| Germination | % |
| Fresh fodder harvested | kg/day |
| Fresh conversion | kg fodder/kg seed |
| Sample dry matter | % |
| Water used | L/day |
| Electricity | kWh/day |
| Labour | minutes/day |
| Mold/rejected trays | count / % |
| Total production cost | NPR/kg fresh |
| Dry-matter cost | NPR/kg DM |
| Fodder fed per cow | kg fresh/day |
| Total ration DM | kg/cow/day |
| Milk | L/cow/day |
| Milk fat/SNF if available | % |
| Feed refusal | kg/day |
| Cow health/rumen/manure notes | qualitative |

Only after 30–60 days of clean operational data should the farm decide whether to scale the system.

## 10. Go / no-go criteria for Cow Farm OS

### Strong reasons to use it

- little fodder-growing land
- seasonal green-fodder shortage
- expensive/unstable purchased green fodder
- reliable grain supply at good price
- sufficient labour/hygiene discipline
- clean water
- system can be built cheaply

### Reasons to keep it small

- abundant low-cost Napier/maize/oat fodder available on-farm
- high maize/barley seed prices
- high mold losses
- electricity/cooling requirement becomes material
- dry-matter cost exceeds conventional alternatives without measurable milk/health benefit

## 11. Cow Farm OS working decision

**Hydroponic fodder is approved for further feasibility design as a supplementary feed module for the 10-cow Phase-1 farm.**

It is **not yet approved as a major capital purchase or a complete fodder replacement**.

Next analysis required:

1. current Nepal maize/barley/wheat seed/feed-grain prices
2. local green-fodder price and/or cost of own Napier/maize production
3. farm location climate and water quality
4. 50 kg/day locally fabricated system BOM and capex
5. 100 kg/day expansion BOM
6. NPR/kg fresh and NPR/kg DM model
7. side-by-side comparison: hydroponic fodder vs Napier vs silage vs purchased green fodder
8. ration integration for the exact breed/body weight/milk target of our 10 cows

### Evidence handling note

Video statements are treated as practical demonstrations/claims. Farm investment and ration decisions should use measured Cow Farm OS data plus NARC/peer-reviewed evidence. Marketing claims of water saving, milk increase or seed-to-fodder multiplication must not be converted directly into financial assumptions without local verification.
