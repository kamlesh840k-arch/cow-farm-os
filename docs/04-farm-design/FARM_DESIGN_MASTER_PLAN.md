# Farm Design Master Plan

**Design capacity:** Phase 1 = 30 adult breeding females; civil layout reserved for 50 adults plus youngstock.

## Site zoning

Keep flows one-directional where possible:

1. Entry / biosecurity / visitor parking
2. Office + medicine + records
3. Quarantine / isolation
4. Calf and youngstock area
5. Main lactating-cow shed
6. Dry/close-up and calving pens
7. Milking/milk room and chiller
8. Feed store + silage bunker + chopping area
9. Manure collection / biogas / compost
10. Dead-stock emergency holding/disposal route
11. Fodder-field access separated from clean milk route

Milk, feed, manure and vehicle flows should not cross unnecessarily.

## Main shed principles

- Open-sided naturally ventilated structure with long axis arranged to reduce direct solar load after site-specific sun/wind review.
- High roof volume and continuous ridge outlet.
- Fans and cow-soaking system installed from Phase 1, not added after heat-stress losses appear.
- Non-slip floor with positive drainage; no standing slurry.
- Adequate feed-bunk access so subordinate cows can eat.
- Multiple large clean-water points; water flow rate matters as much as trough volume.
- Resting space should remain dry and comfortable.
- Expansion bay reserved so Phase 2 construction does not disrupt the milking herd.

## Functional areas

### Lactating cows
Design for 30 initially with space/utilities to expand to 50. Separate fresh/sick cows when required.

### Calving
At least 2 clean individual/multi-use maternity pens for the first 30-cow stage, with direct observation and cleaning access.

### Quarantine
Physically separated pens for all purchased animals; separate water/feed tools where practical.

### Calves
Individual/small-group early housing with shade, airflow, drainage and dry bedding; older calves and heifers grouped by size.

### Hospital/isolation
Dedicated treatment pen and safe restraint area.

## Milking system

Phase 1 target: simple 2-cluster machine or small parlour/milk-room configuration sized for ~25 cows in milk, expandable without replacing the entire vacuum/milk line.

Requirements:
- clean pre-milking holding area,
- washable surfaces,
- hand-wash and udder-prep station,
- reliable hot/cold cleaning water,
- backup power strategy,
- immediate chilling route,
- separate antibiotic/withheld milk handling.

## Chilling

Phase 1 planning allowance: 500 L bulk milk chiller, but final capacity depends on pickup frequency. If buyer collects once daily and herd grows rapidly, choose a larger tank or expansion-ready system.

## Water design

Plan water for drinking, cooling and sanitation. A 50-adult-cow site plus youngstock should be designed with storage and pumping redundancy rather than relying on instantaneous supply. Final tank size is determined after bore/municipal yield test and cooling design.

## Power

Critical loads:
- water pump,
- fans/soakers,
- milking machine,
- chiller,
- lighting,
- office/records.

Provide generator/solar-hybrid/backup plan sufficient to protect milking, water and chilling during outages.

## Drainage and manure

Use gravity where feasible:
- clean stormwater kept separate from manure water,
- scraped manure/slurry collected to a controlled point,
- no uncontrolled discharge to field drains,
- solids/liquids directed to digester/compost route.

## Biogas + vermicompost flow

Fresh manure/slurry -> anaerobic digestion (if final gas/yield economics support it) -> digestate separation/stabilization -> pre-compost/temperature reduction -> vermicomposting of suitable stabilized solids -> cured product.

Do **not** place earthworms directly into hot fresh digestate or ammonia-rich material.

## CAPEX design rule

Build civil works once where rework is expensive: drainage, water mains, electrical trunking, road access, shed columns, expansion corridors and manure flow. Delay optional machinery until volume justifies it.

## Pre-construction deliverables required

- topographic/site survey,
- soil/bearing check where needed,
- water-source yield and quality test,
- flood/high-water assessment,
- electrical load schedule,
- scaled layout with dimensions,
- drainage levels,
- bill of quantities,
- three contractor quotations,
- equipment utility requirements.

This document is a functional design brief, not a structural engineering drawing.
