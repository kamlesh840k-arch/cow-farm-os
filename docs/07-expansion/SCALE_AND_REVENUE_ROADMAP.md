# Scale & Revenue Roadmap

## Principle

Scale only when the existing herd is profitable, fed, housed and managed well. Expansion is an earned right, not a calendar event.

## Stage A — Foundation

**30 adult breeding females**; site prepared for 50.

Objectives:
- stabilize milk quality and buyer relationship,
- prove local feed yields,
- compare HF-cross vs Jersey-cross economics,
- establish breeding/health records,
- produce first home-grown replacements,
- prove cash cost <= NPR 60/L.

## Stage B — First scale

**50 adult breeding females**.

Requirements before approval:
- >=75% lactating fraction,
- saleable milk >=17 L/lactating cow/day for six months,
- feed inventory >=90 days,
- calf mortality and mastitis under control,
- reliable buyer capacity for >=750 L/day,
- adequate heifer/calf housing,
- positive cash flow after debt service,
- no chronic summer heat bottleneck.

Likely output at current model: ~690 L/day saleable milk.

At 50 adults, stop keeping every female calf. Sell surplus quality heifers once replacement pipeline is secure.

## Stage C — Commercial scale

**75–100 adult breeding females**.

Only after Stage B has a full-year stable record.

Potential additions if ROI is proven:
- small TMR mixer/feeding mechanization,
- larger chiller,
- upgraded milking parlour,
- dedicated heifer unit,
- larger silage handling,
- manure solid separator,
- expanded biogas.

Buyer capacity and fodder base must grow before cows.

## Stage D — Integrated dairy platform

**150–200 adults** only if the business has professional management, audited/credible records, multiple milk channels and land/feed security.

At this stage evaluate:
- genetic-stock business,
- contract silage production,
- milk processing,
- branded dairy,
- commercial compost/vermicompost,
- energy recovery,
- training/demo farm.

Do not jump directly to this stage because Banjara has large infrastructure.

## Revenue sequencing

### Revenue 1 — Raw milk
Core cash engine. Optimize fat/SNF, hygiene, consistency and payment reliability.

### Revenue 2 — Surplus heifers/cows
Begin only after replacement needs are covered and health/genetic records make animals credible.

### Revenue 3 — Compost/vermicompost
Use stabilized manure/digestate. Build a real product specification, curing process, packaging and buyer route before counting revenue.

### Revenue 4 — Biogas
First value is avoided LPG/diesel/electricity cost. Treat gas sales as upside only if a legal/economic route exists.

### Revenue 5 — Direct milk
Pilot a limited route after milk quality and delivery discipline are proven. Do not shift all volume away from a stable bulk buyer at once.

### Revenue 6 — Value-added dairy
Curd/paneer/ghee only when:
- raw milk surplus is reliable,
- DFTQC requirements are met,
- processing recovery/yield is tested,
- cold chain exists,
- gross margin exceeds raw-milk alternative after labour, packaging, returns and spoilage.

## Expansion capital rule

Each new machinery item needs:
- purchase price,
- life/maintenance,
- labour saved,
- throughput gain,
- quality/rejection benefit,
- annual cash benefit,
- payback and downside case.

No automation purchased for prestige.
