# Phase 4 — Launch & Stabilization Plan

**Status:** Operating launch playbook complete; execution begins only after Phase-3 commissioning.

## Before first animal arrival

- 90-day roughage/silage reserve.
- concentrate/mineral supplier confirmed.
- water, fans, soakers, milking and chilling load-tested.
- quarantine cleaned and bedded.
- animal ID/record system live.
- vet and AI contacts on call.
- emergency medicines/first-aid per veterinarian.
- insurance active/confirmed.
- milk buyer pickup and rejection protocol confirmed.
- staff complete dry-run milking/CIP/feeding/manure routines.

## Intake SOP

For every purchased animal:
1. Verify ID against purchase record.
2. Record arrival weight/body condition where possible.
3. Physical exam and temperature.
4. Udder/mastitis check for lactating cows.
5. Pregnancy/reproduction record confirmation.
6. Quarantine group assignment.
7. Gradual ration transition.
8. Record baseline milk.
9. Observe feet/legs, respiratory signs, appetite and manure.

## First 30 days

Primary goal: health and routine, not maximum milk.

Daily management reviews:
- appetite/refusal,
- milk per cow,
- mastitis/abnormal milk,
- heat stress,
- lameness,
- rumination/behavior observation,
- water and cooling,
- calf health,
- equipment failures.

Do not make multiple ration, housing and management changes at once unless animal welfare requires it.

## Days 31–90

- establish stable ration by group,
- benchmark feed cost/litre,
- begin reproduction schedule for eligible cows,
- audit milking hygiene and buyer quality results,
- measure actual fodder/silage disappearance,
- identify low-performing/problem animals,
- calculate true labour per 100 L,
- update cash forecast.

## Months 4–6 stabilization gate

Target thresholds:
- lactating fraction >=75%,
- saleable milk >=17 L/lactating cow/day or an approved genotype-specific equivalent,
- cash cost <= NPR 60/L,
- feed reserve >=90 days,
- no unresolved contagious disease issue,
- mastitis/calves/reproduction on defined thresholds,
- buyer payments current,
- no persistent cooling or water shortage.

If targets are missed, **do not expand herd**. Correct the system first.

## Actual-vs-model review

Every month compare:
- herd headcount by group,
- milk volume and price,
- feed quantity and price,
- veterinary/reproduction costs,
- labour,
- utilities,
- receivables,
- calf/heifer numbers,
- culls/deaths,
- operating surplus,
- debt service.

Variance >10% on milk, feed cost, animal mortality, or CAPEX/OPEX assumptions requires a written explanation and corrective action.
